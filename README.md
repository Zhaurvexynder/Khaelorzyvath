# Khaelorzyvath

> Beneath the seventh stair, a crown dreams of the head it never touched.

## The Stair That Refuses Ascent

There are seven stairs beneath the palace, although no palace stands above them. Each is cut from a different kind of darkness. The first smells of wet limestone. The second carries the warmth of an animal recently departed. The third is visible only while someone else is looking at it. No one has described the fourth without forgetting a childhood room.

The remaining stairs are generally omitted from the testimony. This omission is considered a courtesy to the reader, though the reader has never been identified.

At the bottom lies a crown with its points turned inward. It does not fit a human head, a beast's skull, or the empty space between two antlers. Its gold is pale enough to resemble bone. When held near the ear, it produces the sound of a door being opened in a house that has not yet been built.

The crown has no keeper. Nevertheless, someone polishes it every evening.

## A Testimony in Unburned Wax

The oldest witness was a candlemaker whose hands cast different shadows. He descended carrying a candle that had never possessed a wick. He returned carrying its flame inside a closed fist. His neighbors noticed that he stopped using the future tense, but otherwise found his conduct agreeable.

Asked what he had seen, he placed seven drops of wax on a table. Six hardened. The seventh sank through the wood and appeared on the ceiling as a small, patient star.

He said the crown had asked him for a birthday. He had offered the date of his birth. The crown declined. He offered the date of his father's death. The crown declined again. At last he named a day on which nothing had happened, a dull afternoon he could remember only because he had once tried to forget it.

The crown accepted that day. Since then, every calendar made in the district has contained a narrow blank between two ordinary squares. People who put their fingers into the blank feel a cool breeze and withdraw them smelling faintly of roses.

## The Animal Behind the Wall

The stairs have a wall on one side. On the other side there is another wall, but its distance is disputed. Scratches cover the nearer surface. They resemble claw marks until examined closely, at which point they resemble handwriting. No alphabet survives a second comparison.

Behind the wall, something large turns in its sleep. The motion takes several winters. During the first winter, the wells grow shallow. During the second, every mirror develops a small blind spot. During the third, travelers report hearing their own footsteps approaching from ahead.

No one has heard the creature breathe. What passes for breathing is the slow appearance of frost on the crown's inward points. Each crystal contains a miniature staircase, and at the foot of each staircase a smaller crown waits beneath another wall.

The candlemaker refused to discuss the creature. Once, however, he drew a wing in spilled flour. The wing had no upper edge. Its feathers looked like folded shields, and the space below it remained clean even after the table was overturned.

## The Names Lent to Stone

Three names have been spoken on the stairs. None belonged to a visitor.

The first was the name of a river before it discovered the sea. The second belonged to a bell whose sound had been buried separately from its bronze. The third was a name held in reserve for the last person to leave a city. That city remains inhabited, though nobody agrees where it stands.

The crown responded to each name by becoming slightly heavier. The difference could not be measured on a scale. It could be measured in dreams: a sleeper who touched the gold needed one additional night to reach morning.

A strip of silk was later found caught beneath the seventh stair. Its embroidery read:

*Khael — orzy — vath — ennu.*

The silk was buried in the garden. That spring, all the flowers grew facing downward. Bees entered the soil and emerged carrying grains of blue ash. Their honey tasted of an unopened letter.

## Instructions Found Without an Author

The visitor should bring neither a gift nor an empty hand. Both have previously been mistaken for invitations.

If the crown appears to be missing, consider whether you have arrived too early in your own life. If it appears twice, do not decide which one is older. The decision will be remembered by a door you have not met.

Do not count the stairs while descending. Counting is permitted while returning, provided the numbers are spoken in a language you do not dream in.

If a voice behind the wall asks whether the palace still stands, answer with the name of a flower. There are no recorded consequences for choosing the wrong flower. There are also no recorded visitors who chose correctly.

## The Unoccupied Coronation

Once each century, a procession passes above the vanished palace. Its participants wear veils embroidered with windows. Beneath the veils are neither faces nor darkness, but distant rooms in which someone has just extinguished a lamp.

The procession pauses for as long as it takes a mountain to remember an avalanche. Then a hand appears from beneath the seventh stair and turns the crown upside down. Nothing is poured into it. Nothing is poured out.

For one moment, every sleeping creature in the district raises its head. The wells fill with clear water. The mirrors lose their blind spots. Somewhere behind the wall, a wing closes over a warmth too old to be called fire.

The crown remains unoccupied.

The seventh stair remains warm.

And the palace, which was never there, keeps its doors closed until morning.
